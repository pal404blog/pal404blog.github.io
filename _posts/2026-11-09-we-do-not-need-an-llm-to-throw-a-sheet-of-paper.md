Let’s get one thing straight. If your solution to a problem involves downloading an app to throw a piece of A4 printer paper across your living room, you haven't innovated. You've just found a very expensive, highly complicated way to annoy your cat. 

Yet, here we are. The tech industry has officially hit peak desperation. Instead of fixing software that crashes, addressing supply chain nightmares, or doing literally anything useful with our massive data centers, some bright spark looked at the timeless art of the paper airplane and thought, *"You know what this needs? More ai."*

I’m looking at you, Foldable Flight and your ilk. We’ve got motorized conversion kits, aerodynamic simulators, and now machine learning algorithms promising to optimize your crease angles for maximum lift-to-drag ratio. They want us to believe that our childhood attempts—scrunched-up college essays hurled blindly toward a recycling bin—were fundamentally flawed because they lacked a neural network. 

Give me a break. 

The beauty of the paper airplane has always been its radical, analog simplicity. It’s the ultimate low-stakes rebellion. You take garbage, you fold it with varying degrees of geometric panic, and you hurl it. Sometimes it loops majestically and lands on a bookshelf. Sometimes it immediately nose-dives into a cup of lukewarm coffee. That's the thrill! It's chaotic. It's human. 

Enter the tech bros, who look at a moment of pure, screen-free joy and scream, *"Wait! Where is the telemetry? Why isn't this syncing to my cloud dashboard?!"*

Suddenly, you’re standing in your hallway, wearing smart goggles, waiting for a prompting engine to calculate optimal wind vectors so your $50 motorized paper glider doesn't smack into the drywall. We are automating the fun right out of existence. We are turning a five-second distraction into a peripheral device that requires firmware updates.
