Let’s be honest for a second. If you or I tried to loan money to a guy, watch him immediately hand that exact same money back to us to buy our overpriced product, and then called it "organic revenue growth," we’d be sporting orange jumpsuits and listening to a federal judge lecture us about wire fraud. 

But when Nvidia does it? Wall Street pops champagne, tech journalists write breathless profile pieces about Jensen Huang’s leather jacket, and the entire **ai** industry acts like this is just the invisible hand of the market at work.

According to recent reports, the chip monopoly of our era didn't just stumble into a supply crunch—they engineered it. Nvidia has allegedly been funneling venture capital straight into sketchy new **startup** outfits, only for those exact same companies to turn around and blow their newly acquired cash on high-end **gpu** hardware. 

It’s the corporate equivalent of giving your kid a five-dollar bill so they can buy lemonade from the stand you set up in the driveway, and then claiming you're a retail tycoon.

Genius? Absolutely. Con artist levels of brazen? Also yes. And yet, nobody in tech seems willing to say the quiet part out loud because half of Silicon Valley is currently living off Nvidia’s crumbs.

For the past couple of years, we’ve been fed a very specific bedtime story. We were told that the world was running out of silicon. We were told that AI demand was so violently, uncontrollably massive that even trillion-dollar hyper-scalers had to wait in line like plebes for their precious chips. Headlines warned of a desperate scarcity, driving up prices and cementing Nvidia's status as the most important company on Earth. 

Turns out, the scarcity was about as real as a crypto influencer's rented Lamborghini. 

When you finance your own buyers, you control the supply *and* the demand. You create a closed-loop economy where the numbers on your balance sheet go straight to the moon, fueled by capital that never actually left your ecosystem. It's an infinite money glitch, hardware edition. 

The mainstream tech press loves to gush about how Nvidia is "shaping the future." But let’s look at the reality here. We’ve baked an entire global technological shift on a foundation of circular financing. What happens when the music stops? What happens when these government-subsidized, chip-hoarding startups realize they don't actually have a viable business model that doesn't rely entirely on burning venture capital to train chatbots that nobody asked for? 

They go under. The chips flood a secondhand market that nobody wants, and the taxpayers and retail investors get left holding the bag while Jensen sails off into the sunset on a yacht shaped like an H100.

We used to mock the dot-com era for its absurd, circular hype. Now, we’re living through a hardware-store version of it, and we’re supposed to clap because the GPUs have cool neon lights and can write mediocre poetry. 

It’s time to call this what it is: an elaborate, multi-billion-dollar game of three-card monte, played with silicon chips instead of cards. Nvidia didn't just win the AI gold rush; they handed out the shovels, lent people the money to buy the shovels, and then wrote articles about how desperately everyone needed shovels. 

Here is my hot take: Nvidia's circular funding scheme isn't just clever business—it's a massive regulatory ticking time bomb, and the moment the DOJ or SEC actually decides to look under the hood, the entire AI bubble is going to pop with a sound you'll be able to hear from Mars.
